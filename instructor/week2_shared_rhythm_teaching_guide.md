# Week 2 shared-rhythm teaching guide

Use the Commons route as a small, connected week. The canonical AI Fluency and Professional Minds sources remain the teaching authorities; these pages orient students, frame one move, and collect a small receipt.

## Monday — AI Fluency Level 1: Gather Context

- **Purpose:** Students learn that relevant context changes an AI task; more words are not automatically better.
- **Student action:** Run one fictional task with no, some, and rich context; identify what each version forces the AI to guess and one true detail that would only be padding.
- **Evidence:** Context-comparison sheet plus representative responses or the prepared fallback comparison.
- **Say explicitly:** Output and claims still require human verification. Do not use private data.

## Wednesday — Professional Minds: Make It Stick

- **Purpose:** Move students from familiarity to durable retrieval of technical material.
- **Source route:** Open the canonical Wednesday reading and the published Wednesday slide deck in the module.
- **Discussion move:** Close the source and ask students to explain one idea from memory, then name the gap that retrieval exposed. Ask how the same practice would work for an API, command, or debugging concept.
- **Evidence:** The attempted recall, the gap, and one bounded practice to repeat.

## Friday — Professional Minds: Mindset at work

- **Purpose:** Apply the source’s nuanced account of challenge, feedback, leadership, and recovery to computing work.
- **Source route:** Open the canonical Friday reading and the published Friday slide deck in the module.
- **Discussion move:** Compare a response that protects appearances with one that uses evidence, invites disagreement, and develops people. Apply it to a Local AI mistake, debugging failure, code review, or help-seeking moment.
- **Evidence:** One concrete failure/feedback example and the next bounded recovery action.

## The seam across the week

Monday asks, “What information does the other side need?” Wednesday asks, “Can I retrieve and use this without the page in front of me?” Friday asks, “What will I do when evidence shows a gap or mistake?” Together they rehearse context awareness, durable practice, and professional recovery.

## Behind / recovery route

Send students to **Recitation / Get Help** with their goal, attempt, observation, conclusion, and next question. A missing or failed receipt is a starting point, not a reason to fabricate completion.

## Do not improvise live

Do not rewrite the canonical readings, turn the wrappers into book summaries, promise that generated output is correct, or reduce mindset to “try harder.” Keep the source limitations and ask for specific evidence.
