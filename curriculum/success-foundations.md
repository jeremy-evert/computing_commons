# Success Foundations / Semester Kickoff

## Commons role

Success Foundations is the shared beginning-of-semester runway for computing students.

It should centralize the parts of kickoff week that are genuinely common across courses while leaving course-specific introductions, projects, grading details, and disciplinary expectations in the home course.

## Shared outcomes

By the end of the shared kickoff, a student should be able to answer:

- What course(s) am I taking and where do I find the authoritative instructions?
- What is the Computing Commons and when should I use it?
- Where are office hours / Recitation and how do I ask for help?
- What does useful evidence look like in these computing courses?
- What should I do when AI, code, or a tool gives me an answer I do not fully trust?
- What is my current degree/career direction and what is one next action that would improve it?
- What shared professional/AI strands will recur this semester?

## Shared material candidates

The Commons kickoff may stitch together:

- course navigation and rescue paths;
- office hours / Recitation;
- degree-plan awareness;
- career-direction / professional-pathway setup;
- communication expectations;
- evidence / verification habits;
- introduction to AI Fluency;
- introduction to Professional Minds;
- introduction to the Local AI Lab that follows in Week 2.

## What stays in the home course

A home course should still introduce:

- what that discipline studies;
- course-specific learning outcomes;
- course-specific assignments/projects;
- grading and attendance rules specific to the course;
- course-specific technology or resources;
- the first disciplinary task.

## Fall 2026 posture

The Commons version should be treated as the shared source that later course cleanup can point to. Do not delete course-local kickoff material until the Commons path is accepted, deployed, and dogfooded.

## Completion model

During the soft launch, shared kickoff checks may be 0-point/due assignments that record completion and provide a recovery signal without creating duplicate grade weight across multiple courses.

Home courses may retain a tiny application or acknowledgment when needed, but should not re-grade the same underlying shared work repeatedly.
