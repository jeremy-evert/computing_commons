# AI Fluency in the Computing Commons

## Canonical source

`jeremy-evert/ai_fluency`

That repository owns the AI I–V sequence, common-core capability architecture, Monday Moments, source recovery/provenance, and the level-specific production pipeline.

The Commons should **not** copy Monday Moments into individual course folders. It should expose the right progressive level and route students to the canonical material.

## Sequence

1. **AI I — Thinking with AI**
2. **AI II — Improving AI Workflows**
3. **AI III — Engineering AI Systems**
4. **AI IV — Applying AI in Software Engineering**
5. **AI V — Machine Learning and AI Systems**

## Progressive visibility

When Level N is assigned, Levels 1 through N remain available.

Examples:

- CS1 target Level 1 → Level 1 open.
- CS2 target Level 2 → Levels 1–2 open.
- DSCT target Level 3 → Levels 1–3 open.
- Software Engineering target Level 4 → Levels 1–4 open.
- Machine Learning target Level 5 → Levels 1–5 open.

Computer Architecture can consume the level appropriate to the student's progression while adding machine-facing applications rather than forcing a separate numbered AI Fluency rung.

## Shared common-core habits

The current `ai_fluency` architecture identifies recurring habits that should become more sophisticated rather than being forgotten:

- define the real problem;
- gather sufficient context;
- ground important claims;
- verify outputs;
- preserve human judgment;
- document decisions and evidence;
- reflect and improve.

The Commons should make these feel like one accumulating professional practice, not five unrelated mini-courses.

## Monday rhythm

Monday is the natural recurring **AI Fluency** day for the current semester design.

The Commons should provide one obvious Monday route. Home courses can add discipline-specific examples or applications but should not recreate generic AI Fluency lessons.

## Fall 2026 posture

AI Fluency Commons activities may be due/trackable while remaining 0-point during the soft launch. Exceptional application/evidence can earn bonus credit in a home course.

## Migration implication

Course folders such as CS1's historical/local `monday_moments` scaffolding should not become competing authoring homes. Where a course repo needs an AI Fluency artifact, prefer a small pointer, wrapper, or disciplinary application around the canonical `ai_fluency` source.
