# Professional Minds in the Computing Commons

## Canonical source

`jeremy-evert/professional_minds`

That repository owns reusable Professional Minds source material, book slurps, DNA cards, lessons, assignments, quizzes, reflections, portfolio artifacts, and production reports.

`jeremy-evert/ai_fluency` also contains recovered/integrated planning that connects Professional Minds to the five-course AI sequence. Treat that as useful architecture evidence, but do not create a second competing Professional Minds vocabulary in the Commons.

## Commons role

The Commons should make Professional Minds one shared progressive student experience rather than six course-local copies.

Home courses may keep disciplinary reflection/application, but generic Professional Minds teaching should increasingly come from the shared source and Commons route.

## Current weekly rhythm

For Fall 2026:

- **Monday:** AI Fluency
- **Wednesday:** Professional Minds perspective / people / ideas
- **Friday:** Professional Minds practice / demonstration / reflection / portfolio

The exact Wednesday/Friday labels are still open for refinement. The distinction should be useful, not cosmetic.

## Progressive design

Professional Minds should deepen with the student's target level.

Level 1 should help a new computing student build identity, habits, communication, help-seeking, and professional awareness.

Later levels should raise the stakes: judgment, teamwork, leadership, ambiguity, requirements, review, accountability, systems thinking, experimentation, and responsible technical decision-making.

When a student is assigned Level N, earlier Professional Minds levels remain available for review.

## Fall 2026 posture

Professional Minds items may be due and trackable as 0-point Commons activities. Strong reflection, professional application, or evidence may earn home-course bonus credit during the soft launch.
