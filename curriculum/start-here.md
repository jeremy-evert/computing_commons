# START HERE — SWOSU Computing Commons

## What this is

The **SWOSU Computing Commons** is the shared Recitation/workshop for SWOSU computing students.

You may be enrolled in Computer Science I, Computer Science II, Discrete Structures and Critical Thinking, Computer Architecture, Software Engineering, Machine Learning, or another computing course. Those courses teach different subjects. The Commons gives you one place to learn the tools, habits, and professional practices that several of those courses share.

> **Learn the common craft once. Practice it everywhere.**

## What this is not

The Commons is not a replacement for your academic course.

Your home course still owns:

- course-specific lessons;
- course-specific assignments and projects;
- disciplinary grading;
- the way shared Commons skills are applied in that subject.

Canvas in your home course remains the place to check for that course's specific graded work unless your instructor explicitly tells you otherwise.

## Where to begin

### New to the computing sequence

Start with:

1. **Success Foundations / Semester Kickoff**
2. **Build and Verify Your Local AI Lab**
3. **AI Fluency — your assigned level**
4. **Professional Minds — your assigned level**

Your course may also send you to a Recitation Toolbox station when you need a shared skill.

### Returning student

Earlier levels and foundations remain available for review and recovery.

If you are assigned Level 3, for example, Levels 1, 2, and 3 should remain available. You do not need to pretend you never learned the earlier material, but you should be able to go back when a skill gets rusty.

## Recitation Toolbox

The Commons toolbox includes shared skills such as:

- Containers / reproducible environments
- LaTeX and evidence-oriented documentation
- Work First
- Linux command-line inspection and profiling
- Git / source-code management

These are not necessarily assigned on the same week for every course. Your home course may send you to a toolbox station when the capability becomes useful.

## Weekly shared rhythm

For the current design:

- **Monday:** AI Fluency
- **Wednesday / Friday:** Professional Minds, Recitation, reflection, application, or shared computing experiences as assigned

The Commons should reduce duplicate work rather than create a second pile of homework.

## How completion works during the Fall 2026 soft launch

Many Commons activities may have real due dates while remaining **0-point assignments**.

The purpose is to help you stay on track and preserve evidence that the capability works without forcing every shared activity into every home-course gradebook.

Exceptional work, especially strong verification, troubleshooting, documentation, or application, may be recognized with bonus credit in a home course when your instructor chooses.

## When something does not work

Do not treat a tool merely existing as proof that it works.

Useful evidence distinguishes questions such as:

- Is the program installed?
- Can it actually run?
- Can the service respond?
- Can the model actually infer?
- Did the file actually change?
- Did the change actually work?

Bring the evidence you have to Recitation. The goal is not to hide failure. The goal is to make failure inspectable enough to recover from it.

## Recitation

Recitation is the shared help/workshop layer for these skills.

You can come with Local AI trouble, Git trouble, Linux trouble, documentation trouble, container trouble, or a general “I do not know what this instruction means” problem. The important first step is to bring what you tried and what happened.

## The long arc

The Commons is progressive:

- **Level 1:** become a capable computing student
- **Level 2:** become a capable computing practitioner
- **Level 3:** strengthen judgment, evidence, uncertainty, and critical reasoning
- **Level 4:** work effectively in software-engineering systems and teams
- **Level 5:** understand models, data, evaluation, and AI systems more deeply

At the end of the shared sequence, **Farkle + Machine Learning** brings the disciplines back together through code, probability, systems, profiling, models, and evidence.

## One rule to remember

If you are lost, do not guess which duplicate copy is current.

Start in the Commons or the current home-course instruction, then follow the canonical route you were given. If two instructions disagree, bring the conflict to Recitation so the system can be fixed.
