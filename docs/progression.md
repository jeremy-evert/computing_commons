# Progressive levels and release model

The Commons uses a **cumulative level model**.

A student is not placed into one isolated level while earlier material disappears. Instead, the assigned target level determines how much of the sequence is open.

## Release rule

- Target Level 1 → open Level 1.
- Target Level 2 → open Levels 1–2.
- Target Level 3 → open Levels 1–3.
- Target Level 4 → open Levels 1–4.
- Target Level 5 → open Levels 1–5.

Earlier levels remain available for review, remediation, transfer students, nonstandard course order, and students who need to rebuild a foundation.

Later levels remain unopened until the curriculum asks the student to take them on.

## Course targets

| Target | Natural host-course milestone | Primary emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | Computer Science I | Become a capable computing student; foundational AI fluency, common tools, evidence, and help-seeking. |
| Level 2 | Computer Science II | Improve workflows; use the foundation more efficiently and with stronger verification. |
| Level 3 | Discrete Structures & Critical Thinking | Engineering judgment, evidence, uncertainty, assumptions, grounding, and critical reasoning. |
| Level 4 | Software Engineering | Team workflow, requirements, code review, CI/testing, change control, documentation, and professional engineering with AI. |
| Level 5 | Machine Learning | Models, data, evaluation, uncertainty, limitations, responsible deployment, and deeper AI systems understanding. |

Computer Architecture is a cross-cutting technical bridge rather than a forced numbered rung. It deepens the machine-facing parts of the Commons: Linux, profiling, CPU/GPU behavior, memory, containers, performance evidence, and the shared Farkle + Machine Learning experience.

## AI Fluency progression

The canonical AI sequence remains in `jeremy-evert/ai_fluency`:

- AI I — Thinking with AI
- AI II — Improving AI Workflows
- AI III — Engineering AI Systems
- AI IV — Applying AI in Software Engineering
- AI V — Machine Learning and AI Systems

The Commons owns the cumulative delivery experience, not a duplicate authoring fork.

## Professional Minds progression

Professional Minds should deepen alongside the levels rather than repeat the same soft-skills material every semester.

Current Fall 2026 rhythm under consideration:

- Monday: AI Fluency
- Wednesday: Professional Minds perspective / people / ideas
- Friday: Professional Minds practice / demonstration / reflection / portfolio

The exact Wednesday/Friday naming remains open, but later levels should assume earlier professional habits and increase the complexity/stakes of the work.

## Shared technical capabilities

The common technical recitations do not need to be locked to a numbered week after the initial runway.

Capabilities such as Local AI, containers, documentation/LaTeX, Work First, Linux/profiling, and Git can be introduced at the earliest appropriate level and then remain available as permanent workshop stations.

A later course should generally say “use/review the Commons capability” rather than reteaching it from zero.

## Placement principle

Course target is the default, not a cage.

If a student reaches Software Engineering without a clean Level 1 foundation, opening Levels 1–4 gives that student a recovery path. If a student has already completed earlier work, the material remains available but should not require needless re-completion unless the later level deliberately asks for a stronger artifact.
