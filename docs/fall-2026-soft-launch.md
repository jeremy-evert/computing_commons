# Fall 2026 soft-launch posture

Fall 2026 is the semester in which the Computing Commons seam is being proven.

The goal is to centralize common learning without creating a second grading bureaucracy before the student experience is understood.

## Current grading posture

Preferred starting pattern:

- Commons activities may have due dates so students see a real sequence and deadlines.
- Commons activities may be **0-point assignments** for completion/submission tracking.
- Students may earn **bonus credit in a home course** for strong evidence, excellent verification, useful troubleshooting, or other exceptional work.
- Do not duplicate ordinary points across every course merely because the same student is enrolled in several computing courses.
- Do not alter settled course grade weights just to make the prototype Commons look more official.

## Why 0-point matters this semester

This gives us operational evidence without locking the curriculum into an untested gradebook architecture.

We can learn:

- whether students can find and navigate the Commons;
- whether one completion really reduces duplicated work;
- whether students enrolled in multiple computing courses experience less confusion;
- whether recitation hours reduce support friction;
- whether Canvas completion tracking is sufficient;
- what lightweight home-course receipt, if any, is worth keeping;
- whether any safe cross-course grade/completion synchronization is available.

## Bonus-credit principle

Bonus credit should reward evidence or contribution, not mere clicking.

Good candidates include:

- a complete Local AI verification receipt;
- particularly useful troubleshooting notes;
- an independently verified AI claim/check;
- strong documentation that another student could follow;
- a clean Git recovery demonstration;
- a strong profiling/evidence artifact;
- a thoughtful Professional Minds or AI Fluency application;
- a useful Farkle/ML experiment or explanation.

Exact bonus mechanics remain owned by the home course until a shared grading architecture is deliberately approved.

## Enrollment hypothesis

A likely operating model is to enroll students from the participating computing courses into the Commons near the start of the semester and remove/roll students as appropriate later.

Treat that as an operational hypothesis until it is tested in Canvas. Enrollment/removal behavior, access after completion, and semester rollover should be verified before becoming policy.

## Promotion condition

The Commons should become more grade-bearing only when the shared delivery is boring and reliable:

- enrollment works;
- navigation works;
- completion is understandable;
- canonical sources stay synchronized;
- students do not have to repeat common work unnecessarily;
- home-course wrappers are lightweight;
- the instructor is grading less, not more.
