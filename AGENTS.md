# Agent guidance

## Mission and ownership

Keep Computing Commons a shared recitation, clearinghouse, and apprenticeship layer, not a duplicate course or an unnecessary monorepo. It composes the student journey; courses own disciplinary teaching, assessment, and application. Specialized repositories remain authoritative for their domains.

Before adding material, ask whether it is shared across courses, learnable once and applicable in several disciplines, already owned by a canonical source, and likely to reduce duplicate content, grading, or navigation confusion. Keep disciplinary outcomes (such as Python, software design, mathematics, architecture, software engineering, and machine learning) in their course repositories. Course-specific graded applications may remain there as wrappers.

**Stitch, do not fork.** Consume, link, package, or orchestrate canonical sources instead of copying a second implementation. Key sources: `local_ai_lab_setup` (Local AI Lab), `windows_classroom` (Windows classroom evidence), `ai_fluency` (AI I–V and Monday Moments), `professional_minds`, and `swosu_cs_curriculum` (cross-course map). Commons owns the composition and delivery map.

When sources disagree, prefer: current owner decisions here; current Commons composition/progression docs; the canonical specialized source; `swosu_cs_curriculum` cross-course decisions; current course repositories for course-specific truth; Canvas deployment evidence; historical syllabi, old Canvas copies, and archived material as provenance only. Do not revive retired ZyBooks or other stale artifacts merely because they remain in historical sources. Fall 2026 computing material is open-source/course-owned unless a current source says otherwise.

## Course design

- Keep course repositories focused on teaching, assessing, or applying their discipline. Migrate shared AI Fluency, Professional Minds, Local AI Lab, generic Git/Linux/container/documentation, and finale material to their canonical homes or Commons delivery layer. Do not delete or move course material blindly: first identify the canonical destination and any home-course wrapper needed for grading, navigation, or application.
- Levels accumulate: assigning Level N exposes Levels 1 through N for review and recovery; later levels stay unopened until assigned. Do not design a level system that hides foundational material after completion.
- Canvas is a delivery surface. Repository truth should be reconciled into Canvas, not reconstructed from stale Canvas objects. Do not mutate production Canvas, enroll/drop students, change production grading, or assume cross-course grade synchronization without explicit authorization and an appropriate test boundary. The current Commons Canvas course is test/prototype until promoted.
- For Fall 2026 soft launch, favor low-risk experiments: due dates and completion tracking can help; Commons activities may be 0-point; exceptional evidence may earn home-course bonus credit; avoid gradebook design that adds more work than Commons removes.
- A tool installed, model listed, or command present is not proof the student workflow works. Prefer short receipts showing the attempt, result, and independent verification.
- Every addition should explain what duplication, grading, or confusion it can shrink elsewhere; reconsider additions with no such answer.
