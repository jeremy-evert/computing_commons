FRIDAY · PROFESSIONAL MINDS · GROWTH

Resilience and risk competence

Use honest information, connected relationships, participation, and developing competence to respond to challenge.

Do this

  1. Read the canonical Friday article and view the Friday deck.
  2. Identify the three factors associated with resilience: caring connected relationships, opportunities for participation and contribution, and high self-expectations.
  3. Apply risk competence to a technical challenge: gather adequate information, assess the situation, name your competence and limits, then choose a bounded action.
  4. Use double-loop reflection: what should we do differently, and what assumptions, norms, or relationships shaped our response?

What resilience means here

Resilience is not a private toughness score or a reason to blame an individual. The source places it across people, relationships, and communities. Risk competence is not repeating warnings: it joins accurate information with judgment about a concrete situation and the ability to respond.

Honor the source’s limits. This is a companion-source perspective on resilience education, not the unavailable complete book.

Keep the receipt

Record one challenge, the evidence you had, the bounded recovery or help-seeking action, and the assumption or team pattern your double-loop question exposed.

Need help or recovery?

Do not treat a wellbeing or safety concern as a solo technical exercise. Bring the situation and evidence to your instructor or Recitation / Get Help; ask for appropriate support.
