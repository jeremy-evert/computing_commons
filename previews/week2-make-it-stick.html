WEDNESDAY · PROFESSIONAL MINDS

Make It Stick: retrieval practice

Learning technical material means being able to retrieve and use it, not only recognizing familiar words on a page.

Do this

  1. Open the canonical Wednesday reading in this module and read it before discussion.
  2. Before looking back, explain one idea from memory: what makes retrieval harder but more durable than rereading?
  3. Use the slide deck to compare your recall with the source.
  4. Write one retrieval practice you can use for a current programming skill.

Why this matters in computing

When you can only recognize a familiar API or copied example, a small change can stop you. Retrieval exposes the gap while it is still cheap to repair, then feedback gives you something specific to practice. Connect new tools to a mental model you already have, but do not mistake familiarity for mastery.

Let the canonical reading and deck do the teaching; this page is the route and the invitation to retrieve.

Keep the receipt

Record what you tried to recall without notes, where recall broke down, and the next short practice you will repeat. Keep the reading response if your instructor requests it.

If the gap feels embarrassing

A gap is useful evidence, not a verdict. Bring the exact concept you could not retrieve to Recitation and ask for a bounded practice.
